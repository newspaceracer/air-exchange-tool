    What does CADR mean?

    CADR stands for Clean Air Delivery Rate. It tells you how much filtered air an air cleaner can provide. A higher number means the air cleaner can clean a larger space or provide more air cleaning.

    Where do I find the CADR?

    Look on the product box, product specifications, user manual, or manufacturer’s website. You may see several CADR numbers. Use the lowest CADR listed.

    Where to find an air cleaner’s rating
    • Look for the CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) on the air cleaner’s box, manual, or manufacturer’s website.
    • You may see three CADR numbers for smoke, dust, and pollen. Enter the lowest number.
    • If you don’t see CADR, look for an airflow rating in CFM and enter that number.
    • If your air cleaner only lists Air Changes per Hour (ACH), select "Enter Air Changes per Hour instead."
    Need help finding it?
    • Check the front of the box — the AHAM Verifide label is usually there.
    • Check the specifications panel on the side or the back of the box.
    • Check the manual, in the specifications section.
    • Search the model number on the manufacturer’s website and open its specifications page.
    • If it only lists air changes per hour (ACH), also note the room size that rating was measured in — the tool asks for both.
    Get the most from your air cleaner
    • Remove the plastic. Take all plastic wrapping off the filter before using your air cleaner.
    • Close windows and doors while the air cleaner is running.
    • Put it where you spend the most time, such as your bedroom.
    • Give it space. Keep at least one foot of open space around the air cleaner so air can flow freely.
    • Keep it out of corners. Place it along a wall where air can move around it.
    • Using more than one? Place them on opposite sides of the room.
    • Replace the filter regularly. Follow the instructions for your air cleaner. A dirty filter won’t clean the air as well.

    Air cleaner certification

    Since 2010, all portable indoor air cleaning devices sold to people or businesses in California are required to be certified by the California Air Resources Board. See CARB’s list of certified air cleaning devices.
